The challenge

AI expectations are outpacing enterprise reality

Enterprises are under pressure to move faster. Boards expect acceleration. Investors expect efficiency. AI is meant to deliver both. Yet progress remains uneven.

The issue runs deeper than technology. Over the past decade, transformation has become a constant state. Organizations acquired new platforms and new processes, but the way people work hardly changed.

Today, most organizations operate across systems, teams and data at various stages of connectedness. AI enters that environment and struggles to take hold. The result is a growing gap between what is expected and what gets delivered.

The operating model is what holds most enterprises back

The solution

Design for velocity over certainty

Legacy operating models optimized for stability. They separated data, decisions and execution across systems and teams, which limits how quickly insight becomes action. That’s the ceiling AI keeps hitting.

In an era where speed defines advantage, enterprises must orchestrate their people and their AI around experience, the single most important driver of growth and momentum.

The Intelligent Enterprise

A connected enterprise, built to grow

An Intelligent Enterprise acts as one system that senses change, responds quickly and sustains performance at scale. When organizations orchestrate around experience, the operating model changes quickly. AI, data and automation move into the flow of work. Decisions happen where they’re needed. Execution improves with every cycle.

What is an Intelligent Enterprise?

An Intelligent Enterprise uses data and AI as a strategic nervous system, sensing what is happening, understanding what it means, and acting on it with speed and precision. Intelligence becomes part of everyday execution, shaping how work flows, how decisions are made and how value is created.

Insight flows continuously from across the business and its ecosystem. Decisions are informed and accelerated through AI. Execution happens at scale and improves over time as systems learn from outcomes.

The result is a connected enterprise where people, processes and systems operate as one, freeing teams to focus on higher-value work while the organization performs with greater speed, clarity and consistency.

Why are traditional operating models no longer sufficient?

Traditional enterprise operating models were designed for stability, not constant change.

As organizations scale, fragmented systems, manual processes and disconnected decision-making introduce complexity that slows execution and limits growth. An optimization in one part of the model creates drag elsewhere.

That's why executives are always forced into a trade-off between efficiency and innovation.

What are Run, Operate, Grow and Lead?

The Intelligent Enterprise model is structured across four layers — Run, Operate, Grow and Lead — connecting operations, workflows, experiences and decision-making into a continuous system that can sense, decide and act.

How does Valtech approach operating model transformation in practice?

We take an outcome-led, system-wide approach. This starts by identifying where value is constrained, then redesigning how data, workflows and decisions operate together. Improvements are delivered incrementally, building toward a more connected and adaptive operating model.

We begin with a structured assessment of our client’s current state across operations, workflows, data and decision-making. This identifies where friction exists and where targeted intervention will unlock the greatest impact.

We prioritize on high-impact, high-feasibility opportunities — areas where improvements will deliver measurable business value quickly while enabling broader transformation over time.

Do we need to replace our existing technology?

No. We work with clients' existing ecosystems, integrating and optimizing where possible, and modernizing selectively where it unlocks value. The focus is on improving how technology works together, not replacing it unnecessarily.
